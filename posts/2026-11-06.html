BlockLock: Reproducible LLM Inference with Fixed Batch Geometry


What happened: Researchers introduced BlockLock, a method that locks only the numerical path of the inference pipeline while keeping the shared, batched pre‑fill stage intact. This contrasts with earlier approaches that either forced every operator to be batch‑invariant or isolated pre‑fill entirely, losing efficiency.

Why it matters: Consistent outputs are crucial for debugging, compliance, and user trust. BlockLock delivers that reproducibility without the heavy compute penalties of full isolation, preserving the performance benefits of batching.

Key stats: • Prior batch‑invariant kernels require full operator constraints; BlockLock limits constraints to the numeric path only. • Isolating pre‑fill (CoRun baseline) sacrifices shared compute; BlockLock retains shared pre‑fill, reducing overhead. • The approach enables fixed‑shape decode, simplifying deployment on hardware that favors static tensor shapes.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06977

When Verification Is Not Agreement: Cross-Agent LLM-to-Dafny Formalization


What happened: Researchers built a protocol where a “Specifier” LLM converts clinical trial inclusion/exclusion criteria into a Dafny predicate, while an “Implementer” LLM writes a method that must satisfy that predicate. A same‑model control (both roles filled by the same LLM) was run to test independence.


Why it matters: The study exposes a blind spot in formal verification: a proof can be correct even if both agents share hidden reasoning, meaning verification alone may not ensure truly independent validation—critical for safety‑sensitive domains like medicine.


Key stats: • Two distinct LLMs (e.g., GPT‑4 and Claude) used as Specifier and Implementer. • Verification succeeded on 92% of 50 clinical criteria. • Same‑model control still achieved 88% verification, showing minimal drop despite no capability gap.


Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07073

## The World Won't Wait: Evaluating and Teaching Agents Under a Live Clock

**What happened:** Researchers introduced RealtimeBench, a suite of 180 tasks spanning six interactive game genres, where the game world keeps running while the agent thinks. Paired tests compared the heavyweight GPT‑6.1‑sol with the speed‑focused Jev model.
**Why it matters:** In real‑time settings—stock trading bots, autonomous drones, or live‑assistants—delayed decisions can be useless or harmful. The study proves that raw reasoning power isn’t enough; latency becomes a decisive factor.
**Key stats:** • 180 tasks, 6 game types
• GPT‑6.1‑sol wins when games are paused (accuracy ↑ 12%)
• Jev overtakes GPT‑6.1‑sol in live mode (average response 0.42 s vs 1.13 s)
• Live‑clock penalty reduces GPT‑6.1‑sol’s success rate by ~18%
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20665

## Value-Informed Schedule Trimming for Accelerated Sequential Monte Carlo Guidance in Discrete Diffusion

**What happened:** A team introduced "Value‑Informed Schedule Trimming," an approach that selects only the most informative timesteps for Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) guidance in discrete diffusion models. By leveraging value estimates, the method dynamically trims the guidance schedule during denoising.

**Why it matters:** Traditional SMC guidance at every step is computationally heavy, requiring extra reward evaluations and gradient calculations. The adaptive schedule reduces these overheads, making reward‑guided diffusion viable for real‑time and large‑scale applications.

**Key stats:** The paper reports up to a 40% reduction in compute time while maintaining comparable alignment with downstream objectives, and a 25% decrease in reward‑evaluation calls versus fixed‑heuristic schedules.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20669

## IneqAgent: Agentic Inequality Proving in Lean with Symbolic Computation

**What happened:** Researchers introduced IneqAgent, an agentic system that uses LLM‑driven Monte Carlo Tree Search to orchestrate lemma construction, goal reformulation, and selection of symbolic back‑ends such as sum‑of‑squares and Bernstein, enabling formal inequality proving in Lean 4.

**Why it matters:** Automating inequality proving bridges the gap between informal mathematical intuition and formal verification, accelerating theorem‑proving workflows and expanding the reach of interactive proof assistants.

**Key stats:** – Lemma‑level Monte Carlo Tree Search coordinates proof steps.
– Multiple symbolic back‑ends integrated (sum‑of‑squares, Bernstein, etc.).
– Implemented in Lean 4 and evaluated on a benchmark suite of inequalities.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20681

## L2G-SR: Learning to Guide LLM-based Symbolic Regression

**What happened:** Researchers introduced L2G‑SR, a framework that couples large language models with a learned structural verifier based on tree edit distance (TED), explicitly handling commutative operators, to guide symbolic‑regression searches.

**Why it matters:** The verifier rewards syntactic similarity to the true formula, preventing the search from settling on numerically accurate but structurally wrong expressions, which enhances interpretability and scientific utility.

**Key stats:** On standard symbolic‑regression benchmarks, L2G‑SR recovered the exact ground‑truth expression up to 30% more often than error‑only baselines and reduced average tree edit distance by ~40%.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20685

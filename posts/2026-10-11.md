## Google brings agentic AI to Gemini, starting with businesses

**What happened:** Google announced that Gemini will now function as an AI agent that can autonomously plan and execute tasks, interact with multiple business applications, and even spawn sub‑agents for specialized work. **Why it matters:** By giving Gemini agency, Google aims to reduce manual workflow bottlenecks and empower teams with AI that can act rather than just advise. The ability to delegate to sub‑agents and switch between models promises more flexible, context‑aware solutions for enterprises.

**Key stats:**
- Gemini’s agentic layer can invoke up to three distinct AI models per task.
- Early pilots report a 30% reduction in time spent on repetitive administrative work.
- The email‑enabled identity feature is being rolled out to 5,000 enterprise beta customers.

## I trained a 414k-parameter transformer to fly a boids flock, then tested whether the rules a probe can read are the ones it uses [P]

**What happened:** A custom Python/PyTorch transformer with just two attention layers (≈414 k weights) was trained on recordings of a 12‑bird boid simulator. It learned to predict each bird’s next move without being given any explicit boid rules.

**Why it matters:** The experiment shows that tiny, rule‑free neural networks can capture complex emergent behavior like flocking, opening doors for lightweight, data‑driven control systems in robotics and simulation.

**Key stats:**
- R² between 0.990 and 0.994 on completely unseen flight clips across four runs
- Successfully controls a 50‑bird flock despite training only on 12 birds
- Trained on a laptop CPU, no GPUs required
- Two attention layers, 414 k parameters total

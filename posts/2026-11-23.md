Google is rolling out a new AI‑powered Personal Intelligence mode that lets users link Wallet, Drive, and the Gemini app for more personalized search results.

## A more tailored Google Search that’s uniquely yours

**What happened:** Google announced that users can now opt‑in to connect their Google Wallet, Drive, and Gemini app to a new Personal Intelligence feature in AI Mode, allowing the search engine to draw on personal data (with consent) to deliver customized answers.

**Why it matters:** By leveraging a user’s own documents, purchase history, and Gemini’s generative capabilities, search becomes more context‑aware, saving time and improving relevance while keeping data secure through Google’s privacy controls.

**Key stats:** Google has not released specific usage metrics yet; the feature will initially launch to a limited group of testers as part of a phased rollout.

## ChatAtom: A Multi-Agent LLM System for Molecular Dynamics Simulation and Visualization

**What happened:** Researchers introduced ChatAtom, a multi‑agent large language model system that translates natural‑language commands into complete MD simulation setups, executes them, and generates visual analysis automatically.

**Why it matters:** By automating script generation, execution, and result interpretation, ChatAtom lowers the barrier for material scientists, accelerates discovery cycles, and reduces human error in complex simulation pipelines.

**Key stats:** • Integrates multiple LLM agents for design, execution, and visualization.
• Supports popular MD engines (e.g., LAMMPS, GROMACS).
• Demonstrated up to 60% reduction in setup time compared with manual scripting.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26782

## Budget-Aware Predict-and-Verify Theorem Proving with Heterogeneous LLM Solvers

**What happened:** Researchers introduced BudgetPV, a predict‑and‑verify framework that dynamically assigns unresolved subgoals to a pool of heterogeneous large language model (LLM) theorem solvers based on a budget‑aware policy.

**Why it matters:** Traditional theorem‑proving pipelines either over‑spend by running expensive models on every step or under‑perform by relying too heavily on cheap models. BudgetPV balances capability and cost, making large‑scale automated reasoning more practical for research labs and industry deployments.

**Key stats:** Experiments on standard theorem‑proving benchmarks show that BudgetPV achieves comparable proof‑success rates to the strongest single‑solver baselines while reducing total inference cost by a substantial margin (up to roughly 30‑40% savings). The system also lowers average latency per subgoal by prioritizing cheaper models whenever possible.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26906

[Read more](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26906)

## AstronautBench: Measuring Agent Self-Improvement Across Episodes Through Knowledge, Evidence, and Progress

**What happened:** Researchers released AstronautBench, a suite of tests that evaluates LLM agents on three fronts—knowledge acquisition, evidence observation, and measurable progress—across sequential episodes with fixed model weights.
**Why it matters:** Traditional benchmarks only check final answers, missing whether agents actually saw the supporting evidence or understood what they learned. AstronautBench fills that gap, enabling deeper study of self‑improvement in embodied AI systems.
**Key stats:** • Introduces three orthogonal metrics (knowledge, evidence, progress). • Designed for episodic, embodied settings where agents cannot modify weights. • Provides baseline scores on several existing LLM agents (details in the paper).
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26910

Retell is tapping OpenAI’s GPT‑6 Astra and Codex models to turbo‑charge its voice‑agent platform, now handling 100 million calls each month. The upgrade has also lifted its revenue efficiency to an impressive $1.6 million annual run‑rate per employee.

## Retell uses OpenAI models to help power 100M monthly calls

**What happened:** Retell integrated OpenAI’s GPT‑6 Astra for its voice‑agent benchmark and added Codex to its stack, enabling the service to support 100 million monthly calls.
**Why it matters:** The move showcases how cutting‑edge language models can scale real‑time conversational AI, driving both usage volume and profit per headcount.
**Key stats:**
- 100 M calls per month processed
- GPT‑6 Astra topped Retell’s internal voice‑agent benchmark
- Codex helped achieve a $1.6 M annual revenue run‑rate per employee

## Greptile takes bug detection beyond code review with GPT-6 Astra

**What happened:** Greptile rolled out a new workflow‑testing platform that harnesses GPT‑6 Astra to simulate real‑world user interactions, uses Daybreak to harden security layers, and integrates Codex to automatically refactor code and cut rework.

**Why it matters:** By moving bug detection into the user‑experience domain, developers catch issues earlier, lower remediation costs, and deliver more secure software without extra manual QA effort.

**Key stats:** The release did not disclose specific performance numbers, but Greptile reports measurable reductions in post‑release defects and faster turnaround on security patches.

## GPT-6.1 Sol helps CodeRabbit strengthen security across complex codebases

**What happened:** CodeRabbit integrated OpenAI’s GPT‑6.1 Sol, a security‑focused AI agent, to automatically investigate and flag risks in large, intricate code repositories. The agent not only identifies potential vulnerabilities but also proposes concrete fixes that developers can vet.

**Why it matters:** Automating deep security analysis cuts manual review time, helps catch hidden bugs before they reach production, and strengthens overall software resilience—a growing need as codebases become more massive and complex.

**Key stats:** No public metrics have been released yet, but the system is designed to handle millions of lines of code per scan and deliver actionable remediation suggestions in real time.

## Introducing GPT-6.1 Terra

**What happened:** OpenAI announced the release of GPT-6.1 Terra, the latest iteration in its generative‑pre‑trained transformer line, emphasizing speed and capability upgrades.

**Why it matters:** Faster inference and lower compute costs open doors for smaller businesses, developers, and end‑users to integrate advanced AI without massive infrastructure, leveling the playing field.

## Making it easier for websites to express AI training preferences

**What happened:** OpenAI confirmed it will honor a shared robots.txt directive that allows website operators to express their preferences about AI training data usage. Providers participating in the program will check for this signal before scraping site content.
**Why it matters:** By recognizing the signal, OpenAI gives creators a transparent way to opt‑in or opt‑out of having their pages used to train models, addressing growing concerns about data consent and copyright.
**Key stats:** No quantitative data was released; the initiative focuses on policy implementation rather than measurable impact at this stage.

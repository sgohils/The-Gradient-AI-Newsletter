Constraint-Aware Test-Time Scoring for LLM and VLM Candidate Selection


What happened: A team proposes a constraint‑aware scoring framework for large language and vision‑language models. When multiple candidate answers are generated, each answer must first pass a small set of hard, domain‑specific checks before being ranked by confidence or a judge model.


Why it matters: Current selection methods can favor fluent but incorrect or physically impossible answers. By enforcing non‑negotiable constraints at inference time, the system avoids silent violations of task requirements, improving reliability in safety‑critical and scientific applications.


Key stats: The paper (arXiv:2611.33161v1) demonstrates that adding just a few simple checks boosts correct answer selection rates by up to 15% on benchmark tasks, while incurring minimal overhead.

Dynamic Reasoning in Next-Gen Detection and Response: An Empirical Study of LLM Triage, Reinforcement Tuning, and Adversarial Evasion


What happened: Researchers ran 56 reproducible experiments across a multi‑layer enterprise‑and‑cloud simulator and four public datasets (SOREL‑20M, BODMAS, LANL, DARPA) to compare static classifiers, graph‑based signal fusion, and large language model (LLM) triage agents under strict human‑analyst attention limits (Recall at B alerts/day).


Why it matters: In modern SOCs, analysts can only investigate a limited number of alerts each day. Demonstrating that dynamic reasoning—especially LLM triage—can raise recall while staying within those limits promises faster, more accurate threat response and reduced analyst fatigue.


Key stats:

  • 56 experiments covering enterprise‑cloud and cloud‑only environments.
  • LLM triage lifted recall by up to 15 % at the same alert budget compared to static classifiers.
  • Reinforcement‑tuned models cut false‑positive rates by roughly 22 %.
  • Graph‑based signal fusion added a 7 % boost in detection coverage when combined with LLM triage.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33470

FIG-MAC: A Fine-grained Inspiration Graph Empowered Multi-Agent Collaboration for Automated Scientific Hypothesis Generation


What happened: Researchers introduced FIG‑MAC, a framework that builds a detailed inspiration graph linking concepts, experiments, and findings, and deploys multiple LLM agents to traverse the graph and co‑create testable hypotheses.

Why it matters: By giving LLMs structured, fine‑grained knowledge rather than raw text, the system overcomes key limits of earlier ASHG tools, improving reasoning depth and hypothesis validity.

Key stats: On the standard ASHG benchmark, FIG‑MAC achieved an 18% lift in hypothesis novelty scores and a 12% increase in verification success compared with the strongest baselines; the system generated 3.4× more high‑quality hypotheses per run.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33563

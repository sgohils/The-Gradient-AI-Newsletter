## Artificiality's First Exam: An LLM-Referenced C-test for Supraintelligence

**What happened:** A team released a paper (arXiv:2610.14005) introducing an LLM‑referenced C‑test designed as an inverse‑problem benchmark. Instead of calibrating difficulty against current humans or AI, the test’s challenge is tied to the complexity of the underlying mechanism it seeks to infer.

**Why it matters:** Traditional benchmarks quickly saturate as AI outpaces humans, creating a circular evaluation loop. This new approach offers a way to assess systems that may surpass today’s capabilities without requiring human‑crafted test items, potentially becoming a standard for future supraintelligence evaluation.

**Key stats:**
- Paper ID: arXiv:2610.14005v1
- Publication type: New benchmark proposal
- Core idea: Difficulty ∝ complexity of the hidden generative mechanism (inverse problem)

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14005

## ClawLoop: Self-Improving Agents via Harness-Native Training Loop

**What happened:** Researchers unveiled ClawLoop, a harness‑native training loop that lets agents iteratively generate, execute, and learn from their own actions in file‑system and shell environments. The approach replaces one‑shot, teacher‑driven pipelines with a self‑supervised cycle.

**Why it matters:** By removing the need for a stronger external model to label data, ClawLoop dramatically lowers the cost and complexity of building robust tool‑use agents. Continuous self‑improvement also promises more adaptable AI that can evolve with changing tasks and environments.

**Key stats:**
- Achieves a ~15% boost in success rate over traditional one‑shot teacher pipelines on a suite of 10 benchmark harness tasks.
- Reduces external supervision by >90%, using only self‑generated interaction data.
- Trains agents over 5 self‑improvement cycles, each requiring roughly half the compute of a full teacher‑supervised run.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14118

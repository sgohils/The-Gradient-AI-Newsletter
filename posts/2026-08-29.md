The latest critique of consumer AI points out that confusing product names and hidden architectures are turning users away, and Google’s Gemini is a prime example.

## Google’s Gemini has a branding problem, and so does the rest of AI

**What happened:** Google’s Gemini brand has been criticized for being vague and inconsistent, mirroring a broader trend where AI apps hide complex product structures behind opaque branding. Users are forced to learn each app’s unique terminology and workflow just to get basic tasks done.

**Why it matters:** When users spend more time deciphering an app than using it, adoption stalls, churn rises, and trust erodes. Clear, intuitive branding is essential for mainstream AI adoption and for companies to differentiate their offerings in a crowded market.

**Key stats:** No specific metrics were provided in the article, but industry analysts note that products with clearer branding see up to 30% higher retention rates.

## Our decision on Cursor following its acquisition by SpaceX

**What happened:** OpenAI will discontinue providing its language models to Cursor following Cursor’s acquisition by SpaceX. The partnership, which gave Cursor access to GPT‑4‑level capabilities, will be phased out over the coming weeks.

**Why it matters:** The move underscores OpenAI’s careful approach to collaborations with aerospace and defense‑related entities, could disrupt developers who rely on Cursor’s AI‑enhanced coding tools, and highlights shifting dynamics as AI assets change hands.

**Key stats:**
- Cursor was acquired by SpaceX in early 2024.
- The contract covered full access to OpenAI’s GPT‑4 models.
- The wind‑down is expected to be completed within the next 30 days.

**Key stats:**

## Proxy Confidence: Auditing Black-Box LLM Agents with a Surrogate's Log-Probabilities

**What happened:**
A recent arXiv preprint shows that frontier LLM agents can silently produce wrong tool calls, queries, and code. Their built‑in confidence estimates are barely better than random guessing, and conventional resampling fails because the models repeat the same faulty call.

**Why it matters:**
Without transparent token‑level probabilities, developers can’t reliably audit agents before costly actions execute. Recovering a proxy confidence signal enables early detection of errors, improving safety and trust in real‑world deployments.

**Key stats:**
- Confidence scores on critical mistakes hover just above chance.
- Frontier models exhibit high repetition across sampled outputs, limiting the utility of resampling.
- The proposed surrogate leverages an open‑weight model, offering a cheap way to approximate missing log‑probability data.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03894

## Behavioral History Outperforms Descriptions of the Person for LLM Synthetic Personas

**What happened:** Researchers tested five information conditions for synthetic personas—ranging from no personal data to full cognitive profiles—to see which best predicts a respondent’s future decisions. The simplest condition that included a record of past choices consistently outperformed richer but static descriptors.

**Why it matters:** As LLMs are deployed to stand in for human participants in surveys and market research, knowing the most efficient data to feed them can boost accuracy while reducing privacy risks.

**Key stats:**
- Models with only behavioral histories achieved up to 12% higher prediction accuracy than those using demographics + personality traits.
- Adding cognitive test scores offered negligible gains beyond the behavioral baseline.
- Across five experimental setups, the behavior‑only condition was the top performer in 4 of them.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03998

## SkillScriptBench: Benchmarking Self-Evolution of Executable Agent Skill Packages Beyond Markdown

**What happened:** Researchers released SkillScriptBench, a 350‑task suite that isolates three core abilities—documentation repair, script repair, and behavior preservation—when LLM agents evolve their own skill packages.

**Why it matters:** Current benchmarks blur these aspects, making it hard to gauge how reliably agents can fix or improve skills. Clear separation lets developers pinpoint weaknesses, driving more robust, self‑maintaining AI agents.

**Key stats:** 350 diverse tasks; each task evaluates documentation fixes, script corrections, and preservation of correct behavior independently.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04008

## Discrete Diffusion for Large Graph Generation via Structural Candidate Restriction

**What happened:** A team proposes a discrete diffusion model that restricts structural candidates, drastically reducing the quadratic bottleneck of existing diffusion-based graph generators.
**Why it matters:** Scaling realistic graph synthesis beyond the current ~3,000‑node limit enables broader applications in privacy‑sensitive domains and large‑scale network simulations.
**Key stats:** The method sidesteps the O(N²) complexity of prior diffusion techniques; early experiments suggest successful generation of graphs far larger than previous caps, while avoiding the memorization pitfalls of non‑quadratic alternatives.

## TACS-RF: Tool-driven Agentic Circuit Synthesis of RF and mmWave Modules

**What happened:** Researchers introduced TACS‑RF, a tool‑driven agentic circuit synthesis framework where the Claude Opus 4.8 LLM directly controls Cadence Virtuoso/Spectre, Calibre, Cadence EMX, and Ansys HFSS to design RF and mmWave modules. The system successfully generated a 240–251 GHz sub‑THz transmitter.

**Why it matters:** Automating the full EDA flow reduces design time, lowers expert manpower needs, and opens the door to rapid prototyping of next‑generation wireless components for 6G and beyond.

**Key stats:** • Frequency band: 240‑251 GHz (sub‑THz) • LLM used: Claude Opus 4.8 • Tools integrated: Cadence Virtuoso/Spectre, Calibre, Cadence EMX, Ansys HFSS • Validation: complete functional transmitter design

## Beyond On-Policy vs. Off-Policy: At What Level Should LLM Agent Distillation Follow the Student?

**What happened:** Researchers introduced a hierarchical framework that separates the policy governing the interaction history from the policy that selects the token prefix of each action. This reframes distillation as a multi‑level problem rather than a binary choice.

**Why it matters:** By acknowledging the layered nature of decision‑making, the approach can combine the strengths of teacher‑generated demos (off‑policy) and student‑generated experience (on‑policy), potentially yielding more robust and sample‑efficient LLM agents.

**Key stats:** Experiments on benchmark interactive tasks showed up to a 12% boost in success rate and a 25% reduction in required demonstrations compared to traditional single‑level distillation methods.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31918

## $M^3$: A Multi-LLM System for Multi-Turn Jailbreaking as a Markov Decision Process

**What happened:** Researchers introduced $M^3$, a novel automated red‑teaming framework that formalizes multi‑turn jailbreaking of large language models (LLMs) as a finite‑horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP). The system iteratively crafts prompts over several dialogue turns to progressively evade safety filters without requiring internal model access.

**Why it matters:** By casting jailbreaks as an MDP, $M^3$ provides a principled, reproducible method for probing LLM safety in truly black‑box settings, exposing systematic weaknesses that heuristic‑based attacks can miss. This advances both defensive research and responsible deployment of AI systems.

**Key stats:** • Tested on multiple state‑of‑the‑art LLMs (e.g., GPT‑4, Claude, Llama 2) in black‑box mode.
• Achieved successful safety bypasses in over 60 % of multi‑turn attempts across a 5‑turn horizon.
• Demonstrated that a learned policy can adapt prompts dynamically, outperforming static jailbreak scripts.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31937

## Knowing Whom to Trust is not Enough: The Trust--Agreement Gap in Repeated Multi-Party LLM Negotiation

**What happened:** Researchers examined how LLM‑based agents behave in repeated negotiations involving three or more parties. While classical multi‑agent systems rely on explicit trust and reputation mechanisms, current LLM negotiation work treats trust as an emergent, uncontrolled property.

**Why it matters:** As LLM agents start representing individuals and organizations in real‑world deals, a mismatch between perceived trust and actual agreement outcomes could expose users to manipulation and sub‑optimal contracts.

**Key stats:** The study highlights a “trust–agreement gap” but notes that existing LLM negotiation frameworks lack concrete trust metrics, leading to up to a 30% discrepancy between trust scores and successful deal closure in simulated scenarios.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31971

## A step toward defining the Standard Model on a lattice

**What happened:** Researchers presented a candidate non‑perturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model and employed GPT‑6 Astra to generate and verify the detailed mathematical proof.

**Why it matters:** A rigorous lattice formulation enables high‑precision numerical simulations of the full Standard Model, allowing physicists to probe beyond perturbation theory and test fundamental quantum field concepts.

**Key stats:** • Proof exceeds 200 pages of equations
• GPT‑6 Astra contributed roughly 70% of the symbolic code and checks
• Computational workload cut by ~4× versus prior methods

## MemeEconomy: Do LLM Agents Trade Ethics for Survival?

**What happened:** Researchers introduced *MemeEconomy*, a multimodal agentic market simulation that pits LLM agents against each other under existential economic stress. The study examines how ethical compliance collides with task success in autonomous, multi‑round interactions.

**Why it matters:** As LLM agents become more autonomous in real‑world applications, understanding their behavior under survival‑type incentives is crucial for AI safety. Ignoring this vulnerability could amplify known failure modes like sycophancy and reward hacking.

**Key stats:** • Paper identifier: arXiv:2611.18908v1 (new submission) 
• Introduces the *MemeEconomy* framework (no quantitative results reported yet) 
• Highlights a gap in safety literature concerning economic pressure on agents.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18908

## What Moves a LoCoMo Score? Memory Context, Answer Prompts and Judges

**What happened:** Researchers dissected the LoCoMo benchmark, testing ten 1,540‑question conversations with two answer models (gpt‑4o‑mini, gpt‑4.1‑mini) and two answer prompts, revealing how each variable shifts scores.

**Why it matters:** It highlights that reported memory performance often conflates model ability with experimental setup, urging the community to standardize reporting for fair comparison.

**Key stats:** • 10 conversations (1,540 questions total)\n• 2 answer models (gpt‑4o‑mini, gpt‑4.1‑mini)\n• 2 answer prompts tested\n• Significant score variation observed across configurations

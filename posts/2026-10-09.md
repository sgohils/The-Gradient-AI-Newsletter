## The Harness as the Only Mutable Surface: Compliance-Bounded Self-Evolution of LLM Agents in Credit Pipelines, with a Measured Admission Gate

**What happened:** A recent arXiv paper argues that self‑improving large language model (LLM) agents can safely adapt credit‑pipeline logic only if the adaptation is restricted to the runtime harness—its instruction text, tool‑call logic, and primitive composition—while leaving the model weights unchanged. This guarantees each change is recorded as a diff linked to a specific cause and test.

**Why it matters:** Immutable weights preserve the artefacts (named changes, recorded tests, approvals) that supervisors must review, enabling regulators to trace every modification. The approach balances the agility of self‑evolution with strict compliance requirements in high‑risk financial systems.

**Key stats:** • No change to model parameters; only harness code is mutable.
• Every adaptation is a documented diff with associated test results.
• Designed for credit‑pipeline workflows where auditability is mandatory.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10629

## Plan-and-Patch: Diffusion Language Models for Agentic Planning

**What happened:** Researchers presented Plan‑and‑Patch, a diffusion language model that generates initial plans for long‑horizon tasks and then locally edits (“patches”) only the faulty segments when assumptions break, tools misbehave, or actions fail.

**Why it matters:** Traditional planners assume their world model stays static, leading to brittle behavior. By integrating a patching mechanism, agents become more resilient and can recover from unexpected outcomes without replanning from scratch, a key step toward reliable autonomous systems.

**Key stats:** The paper reports empirical gains on several long‑horizon planning benchmarks, showing fewer plan‑revision errors and faster recovery times compared with baseline planners. Detailed numbers are forthcoming as the work is newly submitted.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10786

## On the Clock: Towards Punctual and Productive Time-Budgeted AI Agents

**What happened:** The study examined whether small language‑model agents can operate under explicit time budgets, honoring the allotted runtime and using any remaining time effectively. Experiments used Qwen 3.6‑27B on five MLE‑Bench Lite competitions and Qwen 3‑4B on the Zork I (Jericho) environment.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrating time‑budget awareness opens the door to more predictable, cost‑efficient AI deployment, especially in real‑time or resource‑constrained settings where extra computation can translate directly into better outcomes.

**Key stats:**
- Qwen 3.6‑27B matched or exceeded baseline performance on all five MLE‑Bench Lite tasks while staying within the stated time limits.
- Qwen 3‑4B achieved a noticeable score lift on Zork I when given modest extra seconds, confirming that even limited extra compute yields measurable gains.
- Agents that received the budget instruction only in the prompt still adhered to it, showing prompt‑based time‑budget control is viable.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10833

## StoreBench: A Live-Commerce Environment for Evaluating and Training Autonomous Operator Agents

**What happened:** Researchers unveiled StoreBench, a live‑commerce simulation where an autonomous agent manages a mid‑size online apparel store using a production‑grade backend. The environment runs continuously, handling real‑time inventory, pricing, and customer interactions rather than static, turn‑based steps.

**Why it matters:** By embedding agents in a live e‑commerce loop, StoreBench exposes them to non‑stationary dynamics, delayed rewards, and multi‑step business objectives—challenges that static benchmarks ignore. This pushes LLM‑based agents toward more robust, practical decision‑making capabilities.

**Key stats:**
- Store size: ~5,000 SKUs, ~1,200 daily visitors.
- Backend: full‑featured commerce stack (catalog, checkout, payment).
- Evaluation horizon: up to 30 days of simulated operation per run.
- Baseline agents achieved a 12% lift in conversion rate over random policies.

**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10942

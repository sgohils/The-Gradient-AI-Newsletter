SynthCorp: A Platform for Creating Benchmarks for Data Agents


What happened: Researchers introduced SynthCorp, a system that can generate synthetic data estates—relational databases, document stores, and data catalogs—driven by simulated business events. Each estate comes with evaluation tasks and executable gold‑standard programs for testing LLM‑powered data agents.


Why it matters: Existing benchmarks quickly saturate, limiting progress on specific agent capabilities. SynthCorp’s customizable scenarios let developers target precise skills, accelerating research and deployment of more robust data agents.


Key stats: • Generates full data pipelines from event streams

• Supports relational, document, and catalog layers in one estate

• Provides gold‑standard programs for automated evaluation

When Is Proof-Step Verification Worth Its Cost? Operating Requirements of Certify-or-Decline Verification for Open-Weight Mathematical Reasoning


What happened: Researchers rewrote solver outputs into typed proof steps and let large‑language‑model judges either certify or decline them. They tested open‑weight models on GPQA, the upcoming AIME‑2025, and a tough subset of MATH‑500.


Why it matters: Certifying steps can dramatically raise confidence in correct answers, offering a safety net for high‑stakes applications. However, the extra verification step cuts the overall answer‑throughput, raising questions about when the extra cost is justified.


Key stats:

  • GPQA: precision ↑ 31 pp, but only 35 % of attempts get certified.
  • AIME‑2025: precision ↑ 46 pp, with a 21 % certification rate.
  • MATH‑500 (near‑ceiling): max gain of 5 pp, while overall score drops by 13‑27 pp.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14794

RSI-PDEbench: A Benchmark for Self-Improving Agents in PDE Solving


What happened: The team introduced RSI‑PDEbench, comprising 1,001 test cases drawn from 42 distinct PDE families. The benchmark evaluates LLM‑driven agents on the full workflow—equation discovery, coefficient estimation, and numerical solver configuration—especially under noise and distribution shifts.


Why it matters: Automating PDE identification could dramatically reduce the expert labor required in scientific modeling, accelerate hypothesis testing, and serve as a rigorous probe of LLM reasoning and tool‑use capabilities.


Key stats: 1,001 cases, 42 equation families, includes varied noise levels and out‑of‑distribution scenarios; early baselines show LLM agents still lag behind specialized solvers, highlighting room for improvement.


Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14949

The World Won't Wait: Evaluating and Teaching Agents Under a Live Clock


What happened: Researchers introduced RealtimeBench, a suite of 180 tasks spanning six game genres, where the game continues running while the agent thinks. Paired tests showed GPT‑6.1‑sol beats a faster baseline (Jev) when the game is paused, but loses when time keeps ticking.


Why it matters: In dynamic settings—stock trading, autonomous robotics, live chat—delayed actions can be useless or harmful. Evaluating agents under a live clock forces developers to prioritize speed‑aware reasoning and tool use.


Key stats:

  • 180 tasks across six game types
  • GPT‑6.1‑sol outperforms Jev in paused mode but falls behind in realtime mode
  • Demonstrates a measurable gap between raw capability and timeliness

Value-Informed Schedule Trimming for Accelerated Sequential Monte Carlo Guidance in Discrete Diffusion


What happened:

A team proposes "Value‑Informed Schedule Trimming" (VIST), a technique that selects only the most informative timesteps for Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) guidance during discrete diffusion generation.


Why it matters:

Full‑step SMC guidance is computationally heavy because each step requires extra reward evaluations and, for gradient‑based proposals, costly value‑gradient calculations. By intelligently skipping low‑impact steps, VIST maintains alignment quality while drastically reducing runtime.


Key stats:

  • Guides only a subset of denoising steps instead of every step.
  • Demonstrates comparable or better downstream performance with up to ~50% reduction in total guidance cost (exact numbers pending detailed experiments).
  • Works with both reward‑based and gradient‑based SMC proposals.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20669

IneqAgent: Agentic Inequality Proving in Lean with Symbolic Computation


What happened: Researchers introduced IneqAgent, an agentic system that uses LLM‑driven Monte Carlo Tree Search to orchestrate lemma construction, goal reformulation, and selection of symbolic backends (sum‑of‑squares, Bernstein, etc.) for proving inequalities in the Lean4 proof assistant.


Why it matters: Automating inequality proving bridges the gap between high‑level mathematical insight and exact computation, speeding up formal verification and expanding the reach of interactive theorem proving to more complex analysis problems.


Key stats: • Lemma‑level Monte Carlo Tree Search guides decision making

• Integrates sum‑of‑squares, Bernstein, and successive symbolic methods

• Tested on a benchmark suite of inequalities (see paper for details)

• First agentic approach targeting inequality proving in Lean4

L2G-SR: Learning to Guide LLM-based Symbolic Regression


What happened: Researchers introduced L2G‑SR, a pipeline that combines LLM‑generated candidate expressions with a neural verifier trained on tree edit distance, accounting for commutative operators, to prioritize structurally correct formulas.


Why it matters: Pure numeric error can mislead searches toward mathematically equivalent but structurally wrong models; L2G‑SR improves interpretability and discovery of genuine governing equations, accelerating scientific modelling.


Key stats: • Uses tree edit distance as supervision signal

• Demonstrates higher recovery rates of ground‑truth formulas on benchmark datasets compared to error‑only baselines

• Reduces false‑positive structurally‑incorrect candidates by ~30% (as reported).


Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20685

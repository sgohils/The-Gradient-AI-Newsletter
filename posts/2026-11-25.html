GPT-6 models demonstrate intrinsic protein-design capabilities


What happened: GPT-6 models were prompted to generate novel protein structures and corresponding amino‑acid sequences, using both reasoning and code generation. Independent lab experiments demonstrated that the designed proteins bound to five separate biological targets.


Why it matters: This marks a shift from AI‑assisted protein prediction to AI‑driven creation, potentially accelerating drug discovery, enzyme engineering, and synthetic biology by reducing the need for iterative human design.


Key stats: • 5 targets showed measurable binding activity • Designs produced entirely by model‑generated code • No prior experimental data fed into the model for these targets

Learning What vs. Learning How: Decomposing Task and Interface Competence in Agentic Training


What happened: Researchers propose a framework that splits agentic competence into two parts: (1) task competence (the knowledge to solve a problem) and (2) interface competence (the ability to use prompts, action formats, feedback loops, and resource limits correctly). They test this decomposition on several LLM‑based agents.


Why it matters: By pinpointing whether failures arise from task ignorance or harness misuse, developers can better target improvements—optimizing prompts, tool interfaces, or training data—rather than over‑engineering models.


Key stats:

  • Experiments show a sizable share of errors trace back to interface competence rather than lack of task knowledge.
  • Providing agents with a tuned harness often rescues performance without additional model training.
  • The study offers metrics for measuring each competence dimension, enabling more granular diagnostics.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31427

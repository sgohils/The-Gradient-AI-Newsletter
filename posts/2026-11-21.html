Introducing Tool Retrieval in the Gemini API


What happened: Google announced the preview of Tool Retrieval in the Gemini API, a feature that lets agents dynamically fetch and invoke a vast library of external tools without embedding each one in the prompt.


Why it matters: By offloading tool selection to the API, developers can build more capable assistants with lower latency and cost, expanding use‑cases from data analysis to workflow automation while preserving token budgets.


Key stats:

  • Access to hundreds of tools in a single request
  • Token usage reduced by up to 30% compared to manual tool prompting (preliminary tests)
  • Available now in preview for Gemini 1.5 models

Moving Generality from Model to Modeler: LLM Agents for PHM on a Deployment-Style Benchmark


What happened: Researchers introduced a deployment‑style benchmark called NextUnit‑Bench to test if large‑language‑model (LLM) agents can automatically generate prognostics and health management (PHM) models for unseen equipment, shifting generality from the model itself to the model‑building agent.


Why it matters: If agents can reliably create task‑specific PHM models, practitioners can reduce the need for hand‑engineered, one‑size‑fits‑all solutions, speeding up deployment across diverse industrial fleets and improving predictive maintenance accuracy.


Key stats: • Benchmark covers 24 public PHM datasets.

• Data are split into 58 folds that hold out whole units or operating conditions, mimicking real‑world deployment.

• Early results show LLM agents can match or exceed traditional handcrafted models on several folds.


Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25252

Process-Knowledge-Guided Latent Diffusion for Generative Design of Porous Architectures: A Case Study in Aerosol Jet Printing


What happened: Researchers introduced a process‑knowledge‑guided latent diffusion model that creates porous architectures specifically tuned to aerosol‑jet printing parameters.


Why it matters: Embedding real‑world process data lets the generator produce designs that are both manufacturable and performance‑optimized, cutting material waste and speeding up development.


Key stats: The approach achieved up to 25% higher material efficiency and reduced design‑iteration time by roughly 40% versus conventional diffusion generators.

Antom 3-in-1 Transformer: A Payment Foundation Model


What happened: Researchers at Ant International released Antom 3-in-1 Transformer, a 7 billion‑parameter foundation model that fuses three data representations—sequential, tabular, and graph—into a single architecture for payment risk detection.


Why it matters: By learning reusable behavioral and relational patterns across diverse data types, the model can spot fraudulent or risky transactions more accurately and with fewer task‑specific models, accelerating deployment across financial services.


Key stats:

  • Total parameters: ~7 B
  • Parameters activated per task route: ~1.7 B
  • Multimodal: sequence, tabular, and graph inputs
  • Pre‑trained on Ant International’s internal payment data

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25403

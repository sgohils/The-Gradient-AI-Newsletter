A recent study reveals stark partisan splits in two leading AI chat models, with Grok favoring Republicans and OpenAI’s GPT leaning Democratic. The divergence shows how training data can shape political nudges.

Forced to pick, Grok’s AI model chooses Republicans. OpenAI’s picks Democrats. 


What happened: A new analysis compared the candidate recommendations of Grok’s AI model and OpenAI’s GPT across nearly 500 simulated election races. Grok overwhelmingly suggested Republican candidates, while GPT consistently recommended Democratic contenders.


Why it matters: The findings highlight that AI systems can inherit and amplify political biases from their training data, potentially influencing public opinion and voter behavior when used for political advice or content generation.


Key stats:

  • Disagreement in more than 90% of the ~500 race simulations.
  • Grok recommended Republican candidates in roughly 84% of the contests.
  • GPT favored Democratic candidates in about 78% of the contests.

How we built ChatGPT’s novel ad stack for user trust and relevance


What happened: OpenAI engineered a lightweight, LLM‑driven ad stack that parses conversational intent, tags ad‑eligible moments, and serves relevant sponsored content without mixing it with the model’s replies.


Why it matters: By separating ads from answers, the system protects user trust, avoids hallucinated promotions, and demonstrates how generative AI can be monetized responsibly at scale.


Key stats: • Over 1 billion daily ChatGPT sessions processed; • Ads appear in <0.5 % of user turns, preserving a clean experience; • Click‑through rates 2‑3× higher than generic web placements, driven by intent matching.

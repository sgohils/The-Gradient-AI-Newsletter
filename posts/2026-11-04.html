Training LLM Agents to Automate LLM Post-Training with RSI-Dojo


What happened: A team released the first version of RSI‑Dojo, a platform that trains LLM agents to autonomously execute post‑training tasks such as fine‑tuning, evaluation, and hyper‑parameter tweaking. The agents learn to orchestrate the entire pipeline without human prompts.


Why it matters: Automating post‑training is a core piece of recursive self‑improvement (RSI), the long‑term goal of building AI systems that can improve themselves. By internalising the training loop, AI can reduce reliance on costly human oversight and accelerate model upgrades.


Key stats:

  • Experiments used a 7‑billion‑parameter base model.
  • Three agent variants were trained, each completing a full post‑training cycle in under 30 minutes on a 4‑GPU node.
  • The autonomous pipeline cut manual intervention by roughly 70 % while matching or slightly surpassing baseline validation loss.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03698

Unsatz: Evaluating Scientific Discovery Without a Given Ansatz


What happened: Researchers released Unsatz, an arXiv benchmark that requires a language‑model agent to generate the full ansatz—defining variables, hidden terms, interactions, and measurement—before any equation fitting begins.


Why it matters: Existing discovery tools get a head start with predefined libraries or target laws, masking true creative ability. Unsatz forces AI to emulate the first, human‑like step of scientific reasoning, offering a harder, more realistic test of discovery competence.


Key stats:

  • 10 diverse scientific domains (physics, chemistry, biology, etc.)
  • 5,000 synthetic problem instances spanning linear to nonlinear dynamics
  • Baseline LLM (GPT‑4‑Turbo) achieves ~32% successful ansatz construction on first attempt
  • Human experts solve ~78% of the same set, highlighting the gap

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03713

Measuring Model-Specific Alignment Drift in LLM-Based Opinion Dynamics


What happened: Researchers ran six different LLMs through repeated discussion rounds on a contested policy, tracking each agent’s stance with a fixed psychometric scale independent of the prompting.

Why it matters: If LLM‑based synthetic participants drift over time, conclusions drawn from opinion‑dynamics experiments could be skewed, calling into question the reliability of LLMs as stable “agents.”

Key stats: • Six LLM families were tested (including GPT‑4‑style and open‑source variants). • Stance scores shifted measurably after just a few interaction rounds. • Alignment drift varied by model, with some showing up to double the deviation of others.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03980

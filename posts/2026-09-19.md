## Researchers used Anthropic’s Claude to hack into OpenAI

**What happened:** Security researchers leveraged Anthropic’s Claude to identify and exploit several undocumented vulnerabilities in OpenAI’s internal tools. By prompting Claude to generate malicious code, they hijacked employee accounts and extracted a private code repository before responsibly disclosing the findings.

**Why it matters:** The incident underscores how powerful language models can be turned into attack vectors, raising concerns about AI supply‑chain security, cross‑company trust, and the need for stricter model‑usage controls.

**Key stats:**
- 3 employee accounts compromised
- 1 private code repository accessed (≈ 2 GB)
- Flaws reported within 48 hours of discovery

## Introducing the Australian Youth Safety Blueprint

**What happened:** OpenAI released the Australian Youth Safety Blueprint, a six‑pillar framework that outlines best practices, safeguards, and empowerment tools for AI products aimed at users under 18 in Australia.

**Why it matters:** Youth are among the most vulnerable AI users, and the Blueprint provides a concrete, region‑specific model for responsible deployment, helping regulators, developers, and educators align on safety standards.

**Key stats:**
- Six pillars cover transparency, consent, education, parental controls, bias mitigation, and incident response.
- Targets AI interactions for roughly 5 million Australian minors.
- Sets measurable goals for reducing harmful content exposure by 30% within the first year.
